Sour coffee is the most common complaint from people who just spent real money on "good" beans. It's also the easiest to fix — once you know what's actually causing it.

## What sour actually means

Not bad beans, and not too much acidity — those are different things. Good coffee often has bright, fruity acidity, and that's desirable. Sour coffee is sharp, thin, and unpleasant in a specific way: it tastes like the flavor never finished developing.

That's because it didn't. Sourness is what under-extraction tastes like. Water passed through your grounds too fast, pulling out the sharp, fast-dissolving compounds first and leaving behind the sweetness and body that come later in the extraction. You're tasting the first ten percent of the flavor and none of the rest.

## The one fix that works

Grind finer.

That's it. A finer grind slows the water down, giving it more surface area and more time to pull out the compounds that under-extraction leaves behind. For most sour cups, this single change is the whole fix.

Go one step finer on your grinder than you're currently using. Brew the same way you always do. Taste it. If it's still sour, go one step finer again. You're looking for the point where sourness turns into balance — not sweetness overload, not bitterness, just roundness. That point exists, and it's usually closer than people expect.

## Why not fix everything at once

Most guides will hand you a list: check your grind, check your ratio, check your water temperature, check your brew time. Try to fix all four simultaneously and you've run an experiment with four variables and one result. You'll get a better cup, maybe, but you won't know why — which means you can't repeat it tomorrow, and you're back to guessing the next time it goes wrong.

Grind is the fix that works first because it's the biggest lever and the easiest to isolate. Change one thing. Taste. Only move to the next variable if grind alone doesn't solve it.

## If grind alone doesn't fix it

For most people, it will. If you've gone three or four steps finer and it's still sour, two other things are worth checking, in this order:

**Your water is too cool.** Under 195°F (90°C), extraction slows down the same way a too-coarse grind does. If you're not using a thermometer, water that's rested 30 seconds off a full boil is close enough.

**Your ratio is too weak.** Too little coffee for the water means you're relying on the water to work harder than it can. Weigh your coffee and water rather than scooping — a ratio of 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water is a solid starting point.

Fix grind first, water second, ratio third. In that order, not all at once.

## The takeaway

Sour coffee isn't a sign of bad beans or a bad palate. It's a signal — under-extraction, specifically — and it has one dominant fix. Grind finer, change nothing else, and taste again before you touch anything.
