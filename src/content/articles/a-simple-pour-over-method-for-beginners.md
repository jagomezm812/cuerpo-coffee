Pour-over has a reputation for being fussy: scales, timers, gooseneck kettles, swirl technique. None of that is required to make it better than what a drip machine gives you. Here's a version that needs almost nothing you don't already own.

## What you need

- A pour-over dripper (even the cheapest plastic one works) or a mug-top single-cup dripper
- A paper filter that fits it
- A kettle — any kettle
- Coarsely ground coffee, close to the texture of coarse sand

That's it. No scale required for this version, though [weighing your coffee and water](/articles/the-only-coffee-ratio-you-need) is the next upgrade once this feels routine.

## The method

**1. Rinse the filter.** Place the filter in the dripper, set it over your mug, and pour hot water through it, then discard that water. This removes papery taste and warms your mug.

**2. Add the coffee.** For one mug, use about 3 heaping tablespoons of coarsely ground coffee. Give the dripper a gentle shake so the grounds sit level.

**3. Bloom, then pour.** Pour just enough hot water to saturate the grounds — you'll see it puff up slightly as gas escapes. Wait 30 seconds. Then pour the rest of your water in a slow, steady stream, filling the dripper about two-thirds full, and letting it drain before topping up again.

**4. Let it finish, then remove the dripper.** Total brew time should land somewhere around 3 minutes. If it drains much faster than that, your grind is too coarse; much slower, and it's too fine.

## Why this works without precision

The forgiving part of pour-over is that a coarse grind and a slow, attentive pour cover for a lot of imprecision elsewhere. You don't need to hit an exact water temperature or count seconds on a timer to get a clean, pleasant cup — you need hot water, a reasonable grind, and patience during the pour.

Once this version feels automatic, the next steps — a scale, a consistent ratio, tighter timing — will make small improvements. But they're refinements on a method that already works, not prerequisites for a decent cup tomorrow morning.
